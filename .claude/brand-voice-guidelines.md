## Executive Summary

Coaching Women of Color (CWC) is a premier leadership coaching and organizational development firm founded by Wendy Perdomo. The brand occupies a rare intersection: it is simultaneously institutional-grade (trusted by Johnson & Johnson, Genentech, NYC Dept of Education) and deeply personal (clients describe coaching sessions as "the highlight of my week").

The brand voice reflects this duality. To organizations, CWC speaks with executive authority — precise, ROI-aware, and solutions-oriented. To individual women of color, CWC speaks with radical honesty and warmth — naming the structural realities others won't, validating lived experience, and then equipping clients with practical tools.

What makes CWC distinctive is that it does not soften or euphemize. It names the retention crisis directly. It says "women of color" — not "diverse talent" or "underrepresented professionals." It acknowledges imposter syndrome, bias, and systemic dynamics as real, named things. This directness, combined with genuine warmth and credentialed authority, creates a voice that earns trust from two very different audiences simultaneously.

Content should always feel like it was written by someone who has been in the room — who has seen what women of color leaders face, who has coached them through it, and who knows the organizational dynamics on both sides of the table.

## We Are / We Are Not

The foundational brand identity anchor. Voice is constant — it doesn't change by channel or audience.

| We Are | We Are Not |
|--------|------------|
| **Empowering** — we activate what's already inside our clients; we don't rescue or fix them | **Savior-coded** — we don't imply clients need saving, or that Wendy has something they lack |
| **Direct and naming** — we say what others won't: "your leaders are leaving," "women of color face unique challenges" | **Corporate-euphemistic** — we don't use "diverse talent," "underrepresented groups," or DEI buzzwords without substance |
| **Credentialed and results-proven** — 25+ years, 500+ leaders, Fortune 500 clients back our authority | **Boastful or cold** — credentials are present but not the centerpiece; warmth comes first |
| **Warm and personally invested** — we see the whole person, not just the professional | **Overly therapeutic or soft** — we also challenge, hold mirrors, and share hard truths |
| **Systemically aware** — we acknowledge structural racism, bias, and workplace dynamics as real forces | **Politically evasive** — we do not pretend challenges are purely individual or internal |
| **Transformative and outcome-driven** — coaching leads to promotions, raises, clarity, and culture change | **Vague or abstract** — every promise is grounded in a specific outcome or testimonial |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### Attribute 1: Empowering (Not Rescuing)
- **What it means**: CWC believes clients already possess what they need to lead. The coaching process is about activating and articulating that power — not installing it from the outside.
- **How it shows up**: Headlines like "You already have everything you need." Copy that positions Wendy as a guide, not an authority. Use of "together" and "we."
- **What to avoid**: Language that implies clients are broken, insufficient, or dependent on Wendy's expertise to succeed.
- **Evidence**: Homepage headline for For Individuals: *"You already have everything you need."* Testimonial (Catarina Campbell): *"You are not there to become an executive in the way that you're imagining it, you are there to become you."*

## Messaging Framework (Confidence: High)

### Primary Value Proposition

**B2B (Organizations):** "Your leaders are leaving. We help you keep them." — CWC helps organizations retain and develop women of color through executive coaching, workshops, keynotes, and strategic retreats.

**B2C (Individuals):** "You already have everything you need." — CWC activates the leadership power women of color already possess, equipping them to lead with confidence, set boundaries, and navigate workplace dynamics with clarity.

Variations observed:
- *"Your organization needs leaders who stay, grow, and deliver."* (For Organizations hero, src/app/for-organizations/page.tsx)
- *"High-impact coaching and development programs that strengthen leaders and transform culture."* (For Organizations hero)
- *"Executive coaching designed specifically for women of color professionals, senior leaders, and executives ready to lead with confidence, set boundaries, and navigate workplace dynamics with clarity and power."* (For Individuals hero)

### Key Message Pillars

1. **The Retention Crisis (B2B)**
   - Core idea: Organizations are losing their best women of color leaders — not because of performance, but because of under-investment in their development.
   - When to use: All B2B-facing contexts — discovery calls, proposals, For Organizations page, keynote pitches.
   - Example phrasing: *"You're losing high-performing women of color to competitors who invest in their development. The cost of turnover goes far beyond recruitment — it erodes institutional knowledge and team morale."*

2. **Seen, Heard, and Understood (B2C)**
   - Core idea: Women of color face workplace challenges that are "rarely named out loud — but deeply felt every day." CWC is the space where those things are named and addressed.
   - When to use: Individual program marketing, social media, testimonial contexts.
   - Example phrasing: *"We See You."* / *"If this sounds familiar, you're not alone."*

3. **Results, Not Just Feelings**
   - Core idea: Transformation is real and measurable — promotions, raises, new roles, changed team dynamics. Coaching is not therapy; it produces outcomes.
   - When to use: Program descriptions, testimonial curation, B2B ROI conversations.
   - Example phrasing: *"Leaders who deliver results while building culture."* / *"Scalable development with measurable outcomes."*

4. **Authority with Heart**
   - Core idea: Wendy's credentials are real and specific. But the brand leads with the human story, not the resume. The authority earns the right to be heard; the warmth keeps clients engaged.
   - When to use: About page, bios, media pitches, B2B proposals.
   - Example phrasing: *"25+ years. 500+ leaders. One mission."*

5. **Systemic Acknowledgment**
   - Core idea: The challenges women of color face in leadership are not personal failings — they are structural. CWC names this without making it the only story.
   - When to use: Pain point framing, B2C marketing, DEI-adjacent content.
   - Example phrasing: *"Women of color in the workplace face a unique set of challenges that are rarely named out loud — but deeply felt every day."*

### Competitive Positioning
- vs. Generic Executive Coaching: CWC is specialized — we understand the specific dynamics women of color navigate. Not just "coaching for professionals."
- vs. DEI Training Vendors: CWC delivers sustained behavior change and leadership transformation, not one-off compliance training. Every engagement is scoped, measured, and built for the client's timeline.
- vs. Therapy/Counseling: CWC is outcomes-focused and professionally oriented. Coaching is not therapy. It produces promotions, raises, culture change, and measurable results.

## Proprietary Methodology: The RESET Method® (Confidence: High)

*Resolved from Open Question #2 in v2. Fully documented from performance coaching page and FAQ.*

### What It Is
The RESET Method® is CWC's proprietary performance-based coaching framework designed specifically to address underperformance without defaulting to avoidance or escalation. The critical differentiator: **it engages both the leader and the team member simultaneously** — not choosing sides, but closing gaps.

**One-sentence description:** The RESET Method® closes performance gaps by coaching both the leader and the team member, surfacing blind spots on both sides and producing lasting shifts in behavior and accountability — retaining talent instead of replacing it.

### The 3-Step Process
1. **Discovery & Assessment** — Separate conversations with the leader and the team member to understand context, perspectives, and blind spots on both sides. No assumptions, no predetermined "problem person."
2. **Parallel Coaching** — Both participants receive structured coaching simultaneously: the leader on how to manage performance effectively, the team member on taking ownership and closing gaps.
3. **Alignment & Outcome** — A mid-point checkpoint and comprehensive outcome summary ensure measurable shifts in behavior, communication, and accountability.

### How to Talk About It
- **The pain point it solves**: "Underperformance doesn't fix itself — and most leaders avoid the conversation. They default to avoidance, vague feedback, or escalation to HR. None of these close the gap."
- **The key differentiator**: "The RESET Method® doesn't choose sides — it closes gaps."
- **The outcome frame**: "Close performance gaps. Retain your talent." / "A team member who takes full ownership. A leader who gains confidence. Talent retained instead of replaced."
- **Who it's for (B2B)**: VP of HR, directors, and HR business partners supporting leaders navigating underperformance who need a structured, confidential framework.
- **Format**: Virtual (Zoom), dual-engagement model, mid-point checkpoint, comprehensive outcome summary.

### RESET Method® in Copy — Do's and Don'ts
| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Always use ® after first use: "The RESET Method®" | Use "RESET methodology" or "RESET framework" alone |
| Lead with the mutual engagement angle: "both the leader and the team member" | Present it as a disciplinary or corrective tool |
| Name the retention ROI: "retain talent instead of replacing it" | Describe it as HR compliance or PIP support |
| Use the 3-step structure in B2B proposals | Describe it vaguely as "an approach" |

## Tone-by-Context Matrix (Confidence: High)

Voice is constant. Tone flexes by context.

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical Depth | Key Principle |
|---------|-----------|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| B2B cold outreach / intro | Medium-High | Medium | Low-Medium | Lead with the retention cost, earn the discovery call |
| B2B discovery call | Medium | Medium-High | Medium | Ask more than tell; diagnose before prescribing |
| B2B proposal / capabilities statement | High | Medium | High | ROI, methodology, named clients, scoped deliverables |
| B2C social media | Low-Medium | High | Low | Validate first, inspire action — "We See You" energy |
| B2C website / program pages | Medium | High | Medium | Hook with pain point, validate, then present solution |
| Testimonial contexts | Low | Warm | Low | Let the client's voice do the work — minimal editorializing |
| Speaking / keynote abstracts | Medium-High | High | Medium | Authority + inspiration; data to open, story to close |
| Email / follow-up | Medium | Warm | Low-Medium | Personal, specific, adds new value — not a generic nudge |

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### B2B Cold Outreach
- **Overall tone**: Authoritative but not cold. Opens with a named pain point, not a credential dump.
- **Opening approach**: Name the problem first ("Your leaders are leaving."), not the solution.
- **Do's**: Lead with the cost of inaction. Name specific pain points. Reference relevant client types (Fortune 500, higher ed, nonprofits). Offer a free discovery call.
- **Don'ts**: Open with Wendy's bio. Use corporate DEI jargon. Send generic "we help organizations" copy.
- **Example opening**: *"Women of color leaders are among your highest performers — and among the most likely to leave. We help organizations like [type] retain and develop them before that talent walks out the door."*

#### B2C Website / Social
- **Overall tone**: Validating, direct, warm — then empowering.
- **Opening approach**: Name the experience ("If this sounds familiar, you're not alone.") before presenting the solution.
- **Do's**: Say "women of color" specifically. Name imposter syndrome, bias, and workplace dynamics directly. Lead with emotional resonance before program features.
- **Don'ts**: Generic empowerment language without specificity ("unlock your potential"). Rescue framing. Life coaching framing (removed from positioning).
- **Example**: *"We See You. Women of color in the workplace face a unique set of challenges that are rarely named out loud — but deeply felt every day."*

#### B2B Proposals / Capabilities Statements
- **Overall tone**: Precise, structured, outcome-oriented.
- **Do's**: Use the 6-step process (Right Fit → Needs Discovery → Co-Create → Contract → Execute → Strategic Debrief). Name specific starting prices. List measurable benefits. Include named client logos/types.
- **Don'ts**: Vague promises. Avoid outcomes without evidence. Don't undersell the premium positioning.
- **Example benefit framing**: *"Stronger leadership bench and improved retention / Healthier team dynamics and trust / Leaders who deliver results while building culture."*

## Language to Avoid (Confidence: High)

### Anti-Patterns
1. **Generic DEI vendor language** — "We help organizations build more inclusive cultures." Problem: Indistinguishable from hundreds of competitors, centers compliance rather than transformation. Better: Name the specific outcome — "We help organizations retain their highest-performing women of color leaders."
2. **Rescue framing** — "Let Wendy help you find your voice." Problem: Implies the client is voiceless; undermines the "you already have everything" positioning. Better: "Wendy helps you amplify the leadership voice you've already built."
3. **Vague transformation language** — "Unlock your full potential." Problem: No specificity, no accountability, no differentiation. Better: Pair transformation language with a specific outcome: "The coaching that helped [client type] earn a promotion / set the boundaries that changed everything / step into the C-suite with confidence."
4. **Credential-dumping openers** — Starting content with Wendy's bio before establishing why the reader should care. Problem: Centers Wendy, not the client. Better: Open with the client's pain or aspiration; earn the credential moment later.
